    • What It Is

    What is subliminal messaging?

    A message received by a person, subconsciously.

    In my own words

    Subliminal messaging in advertising utilizes a person's psychological traits (like emotions, impulse, reasoning, etc.) to expose them to a brand's image, like the product or "message," without their knowledge. Subliminal messaging is able to expose a person to certain things without the conscious knowledge that they are being exposed to those things.

    Types of Stimuli

    Does subliminal messaging (in marketing) actually work?

    Results vary significantly, despite there being hundreds of studies conducted on this concept.1

    Subliminal messaging can change how you feel, what you do, and how/what you think about something. However, only certain kinds of messages can do this, and only under certain conditions. (Very fragile)

    Type 1  ·  Direct instruction
    Telling people what to do, straight up.

    Phrases like “BUY POPCORN” or “DRINK COKE” have been shown to do nothing at all, or at most something very weak (insignificant enough that nobody could get it to happen again).1

    Type 2  ·  Drive or affect
    Hinting at something the consumer already feels.

    Not a direct order, but rather a “hint” or type of feeling associated with the brand. Most of the studies that got strong, repeatable results were doing this, under the right conditions.1

    Type 3  ·  Lexical primes
    Pushing a certain word for repeated recognition.

    Similar to the overarching theme of subliminal messaging, but instead uses smaller words as hints, slowly associating those words with certain feelings towards a brand (although considered “weak” by some researchers).1

    The general pattern
    Giving a direct order versus leading with emotion.

    Mindlessly pushing something, versus pushing with intention.1

    How It Affects People

    Does it affect everyone the same way?

    The message lands differently, depending on the person.2

    Anxious people tend to pick up emotion-driven words more than plain (emotionless) ones, as do people who are more open and less guarded.2

    The question of “does it work” in the context of clothing brand marketing is wrong. Clothing brands get to pick their audience, so “who does it work on” is much more effective, and accurate.

    Short-Form Content

    What happens when the video is three seconds long?

    Your brain decides whether it likes an ad before you've even thought about it.

    A 2024 fMRI study published in the Journal of Marketing Research found that neural signals tied to emotion begin predicting whether someone will like a video ad within three seconds of watching. Social cognition (the part of your brain that reads other people) came in, then executive function (the part of the brain that thinks critically about a situation). This came last, and is the opposite of “liking” something. In other words, the more you think about it, the less you like it.12

    That matters because short-form content doesn't give the executive function any time to show up. In this case, a three-second TikTok or Instagram Reel can land the emotional hook before the critical part of your brain has even started to process it, which is why social media is such a powerful tool in advertising (but also very hard).

    A separate 2024 peer-reviewed study ran a two-week diary study with regular TikTok and Instagram Reels users, where every single participant said that short videos had changed their views on topics they had already considered important to them. The majority of the time, the participants didn't look for those videos. Instead, they encountered them passively through their FYP (or other forms of that), and most of them didn't even fact-check what they saw.13

    Neither of these studies uses the word “subliminal” (in the context of marketing). But the pattern they describe (an emotional response landing before critical thinking can catch up), delivered through short-form content, relates back to the core concept of subliminal messaging in marketing: experiencing something without consciously knowing.

    Applied to marketing

    How does this apply to marketing?

    Instead of the brand communicating to the consumer, it becomes the consumer's personality.2

    Clothing can be a statement about who you are as a person, making it all the more effective.

    In my own words

    Subliminal messaging in the streetwear space takes advantage of a person's emotional state, subconsciously exposing them to information about a certain brand. In the context of social media marketing for a clothing brand (in the streetwear space), subliminal messaging can be used to make the consumer feel/associate a certain feeling or emotion to the brand, tying them or making them feel like they "belong" to that brand (community).

    Has this ever actually been used?

    (Outside of the modern marketing space)

    In September of 2000, a George W. Bush campaign ad was accused of showing his opponent Al Gore's face while pieces of the words “bureaucrats” and “democrats” flashed on screen repeatedly.3 This technique is called evaluative conditioning: when an ad or piece of media pairs two things together enough times, the feeling from one rubs off on the other, establishing a negative or positive connotation with the consumer.

    Case Study

    How do Corteiz's methods relate to the research?

    In my own words

    Strategies used in this type of marketing (hype-based clothing) rely heavily on applying subconscious feeling amongst the consumer, leading them to willingly follow a brand, establishing something of a "cult," or community surrounding the brand.

    Clint never gave an “order.”

    Clint went the complete opposite of typical brands' advertising. Instead of asking for people's attention, he made them ask him for his, making it as hard as possible to buy his product.

    Swapping one identity for another (Da Great Bolo Exchange)

    Swapping something like a North Face coat for a coat they had never seen before didn't make sense, but swapping something with no emotion for something with emotion did.

    He gets to choose his audience.

    Since Clint's method doesn't work on everyone, he does as much as he can to get the attention of the people who are already interested. As a result, the format does his targeting for him.2

    Corteiz isn't “fully” subliminal.

    Based on the sources and my own understanding of subliminal messaging, Clint's marketing strategy is a mix of supraliminal and subliminal messaging, leaning more towards supraliminal.2

    What my data doesn't prove

    When sorting my posts, I found that all 27 captions asked for something: “Join SMS,” “Dropping 11/5,” “Out now,” etc. There wasn't an indirect (subliminal) group to compare any of this against.

    My most direct organic campaign was my best organic drop, out of the three organic drops I did. However, this time around, the drop was supraliminal, meaning that I was visibly stating or asking something in each post. Next time around, it'll be interesting to see if I can incorporate subliminal messaging instead.

    Ethics & Next Steps

    Is This Ethical?

    Researchers question it, but don't give a concrete answer (yes/no).

    Research on perception without awareness has provoked strong reactions from people inside and outside science, largely because of how easily the technique could be abused, and how “easily” it can be replicated.1

    In my own words

    The “darker” tactics of subliminal messaging in marketing can be intentionally manipulative, in multiple ways. Researchers also pose ethical concerns regarding subliminal marketing, providing examples of real-world scenarios (around politics and other fields). Sets up the core concept for modern clothing brand marketing, as well as general advertising, and poses questions of "Do we really want consciousness to be in charge?"

    In my own words

    In the case of community building within modern streetwear clothing brands, the main way of establishing a base like this is through subliminal messaging, subconsciously convincing the consumer that they belong in that community, for whatever reason. To fully understand how and why brands choose to use subliminal messaging as a way of "propaganda" in recruiting consumers, I first have to understand what causes subliminal messaging and stimuli to be so effective and convincing.

    Unanswered Questions

    An assumption I had to drop
    Dopamine was involved

    Before reading anything I wrote down that this "involves neurotransmitters like dopamine." None of my sources mention dopamine, or any type of neurotransmitter.

    Question 1  ·  partly answered
    Behaviour, not mechanism.

    My sources describe what subliminal messaging does to people, but none of them explain what the brain is physically doing while it happens (less neuro-heavy research, and more behavioral).

    Question 2  ·  partly answered
    Both studies used are not directly about subliminal messaging.

    One study shows the brain decides on ads in three seconds, whilst the other shows short videos change views passively. Neither of them uses the word “subliminal,” but the pattern lines up with the overarching concept. Out of what I could find, no study looks at short-form content and subliminal messaging together.
